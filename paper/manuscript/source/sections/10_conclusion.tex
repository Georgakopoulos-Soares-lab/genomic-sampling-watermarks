\section{Conclusion}

We asked whether an exact-marginal keyed sampler can carry provenance through a fixed 6-mer genomic
language model to a standalone verifier, and measured the answer on three released generation policies.

It can, within limits that we can now state precisely rather than estimate. Detection is complete from
$\shortestDetectedBases{}$ bases at a false-positive rate calibrated over every alignment the detector
searches, and it survives roughly one base in five being substituted, arbitrary cropping, and reverse
complementation. The channel that carries it is $\capacityLow{}$ to $\capacityHigh{}$ bit per base,
bounded at $\capacityCeiling{}$ by the construction rather than by the models.

Three limits are sharper than the capability. Insertions and deletions defeat the detector one to two
orders of magnitude below its substitution tolerance, because a fixed-block tokenizer turns one edited
base into a corrupted remainder; a declared windowed search moves that limit and then meets a wall no
search can move. Measured against the two published exact-marginal alternatives on identical footing,
our construction is the weaker one, and structurally so: it couples one bit per token and is already at
that ceiling. And key reuse defeats it outright --- $\forgeryDonorsRequired{}$ outputs under one key
suffice to mint a sequence the verifier accepts, because the statistic is content-blind at every
position.

The last of those is the one that should shape what comes next. A watermark that authenticates a
distribution rather than a sequence is useful for asking "did this come from a keyed generator" and
useless for asking "did this particular sequence come from me". Closing that gap needs a construction
that binds the mark to the content, and this paper does not offer one.

We also report where our own instruments run out. A bounded rearrangement removes the watermark, and
after implementing both order-sensitive measures we specified for the purpose, neither prices it: the
independent-model score barely moves, and the reading-frame measure moves without direction, because a
long frame in high-entropy DNA is a chance event that permutation re-rolls. That is an open measurement
problem, not a solved one.

What we would keep from the method rather than the result is the discipline. Every printable number
passed through a ledger, every figure and table is generated from it, and seven times an unsigned
magnitude looked like an effect until a paired test dissolved it. Two of this paper's four main results
are negative, and both were found by looking for them.
