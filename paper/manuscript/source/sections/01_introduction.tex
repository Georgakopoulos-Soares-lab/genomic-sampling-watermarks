\section{Introduction}

Autoregressive genomic language models create a provenance problem: a generated DNA sequence can be copied or edited after leaving the model, while ordinary metadata can be removed. Sampling-time watermarking may embed a keyed statistical signal without retraining the model. Fixed 6-mer tokenization provides a large nominal alphabet, but the usable channel depends on realized probability mass and is fragile to nucleotide-level synchronization errors.

We ask whether an exact-marginal keyed sampler can support standalone verification across two genomic model families and under a declared DNA edit channel. Our contribution is an empirical and methodological study, not a claim of a new cryptographic primitive.
