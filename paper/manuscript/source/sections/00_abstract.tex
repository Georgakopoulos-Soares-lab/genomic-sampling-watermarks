\begin{abstract}
We study secret-key, distribution-preserving sampling watermarks for genomic language models that
emit fixed 6-mer DNA tokens, where a standalone verifier holds only a DNA sequence, a runtime key, and
public configuration. On three released generation policies from two model families we measure the
realized watermark channel, confirm exact one-step marginal preservation, and calibrate a model-free
detector over its complete declared search of both strand orientations, all six blocking phases, and
eight key-stream offsets. Four results follow, and two of them are negative. First, the usable channel
is $\capacityLow{}$ to $\capacityHigh{}$ bit per base, capped at $\capacityCeiling{}$ by the
construction rather than by the models, so the nominal $4{,}096$-token alphabet overstates the channel
by more than an order of magnitude. Second, clean detection is complete from
$\shortestDetectedBases{}$ bases and survives $\substitutionTolerance{}$ substitutions per base, but
insertions and deletions defeat the detector one to two orders of magnitude earlier, because one
edited base moves every later token off the reading grid; a declared sliding-window search moves that
limit and then meets an information-theoretic wall. Third, measured against the two published
exact-marginal alternatives on the same corpus and the same calibrated detector, our construction is
\emph{not} the strongest: exponential sampling carries at least $\expSignalRatio{}$ times its
per-token signal, because partition coupling is at its structural ceiling of one bit per token.
Fourth, key reuse defeats the construction outright: an attacker holding
$\forgeryDonorsRequired{}$ outputs under one key can splice a sequence the generator never produced
and the verifier accepts it at rate $\forgeryDetectionRate{}$, because the statistic is a sum of
content-blind per-position checks. The construction authenticates a distribution, not a sequence. We
report all four outcomes with the evidence that supports them, and state which claims are
mathematical, which are empirical, and which are biological proxies.
\end{abstract}
